Boards, 3D models & block diagrams

Each project's Hardware tab holds everything physical about the product: its boards, its enclosure and other mechanical parts, and block diagrams at three levels.

Boards

A product often has several PCBs — for example a power board, a control board and a front panel or I/O board. Add each one under Hardware → Add board. Every board has its own revisions (Rev A, Rev B…), and every revision its own BOM, design files, 3D model, release checklist and builds. A product with a single PCB just has one board.

Board revisions appear everywhere with the board's name — "PWR Control board Rev A" — so tasks, builds and firmware compatibility always point at the right PCB.

3D models of boards

Export the board's 3D model from KiCad and upload it to the revision's Design files:

  • STEP — File → Export → STEP (best: real component models and colours).
  • VRML (.wrl) — File → Export → VRML.
  • glTF (.glb) — File → Export → glTF (KiCad 8 and later).

It's shown on the revision and board pages. In CI, kicad-cli pcb export step (or KiBot) can produce it automatically.

Enclosures and mechanical parts

Under Hardware → Add part, add the enclosure, lids, brackets, bezels, light pipes and so on. Each part has a revision letter, a status (Concept → Prototype → Released → Obsolete), a manufacturing process, material and finish, and the boards it holds.

Upload its files: the CAD source (SolidWorks, Fusion 360, Inventor…), a STEP or 3MF export, print files and drawings. Every upload of the same name becomes a new version. Released parts are locked; the project lead can reopen them.

The 3D viewer

Viewable formats: STEP / STP, IGES, STL, 3MF, OBJ, glTF / GLB and VRML. Native files from SolidWorks (.sldprt, .sldasm), Fusion 360 (.f3d), Inventor, CATIA, Creo and others are stored and versioned but open only in their own program — upload a STEP or 3MF export next to them to view them here.

  • Drag to rotate, right-drag (or Shift+drag) to move, scroll to zoom, double-click to centre on a point. On a phone: one finger rotates, two fingers zoom.
  • 3D / Top / Front / Side / Bottom views, Fit, and Ortho for a view without perspective.
  • Section cuts through the model along X, Y or Z — use it to check how boards sit in the enclosure.
  • Measure: click two points to get the distance and ΔX / ΔY / ΔZ in millimetres.
  • Parts lists the parts of an assembly; untick one to hide it.
  • PNG saves a picture of the current view.

Large STEP files are prepared in the background and can take a minute the first time; after that they open instantly.

Block diagrams

Hardware teams usually keep three levels of block diagram:

  • System (on the Hardware tab): the whole product — its boards and external parts, and the power, buses and cables between them.
  • High-level (per board): the main functional blocks — power path, processor, memory, sensors, interfaces.
  • Detailed (per board): real ICs with part numbers, power rails with voltages and currents, buses with addresses and pins.

New diagrams can start from a starter layout, a copy of another diagram (e.g. the high-level one when you start the detailed one), or an empty page.

Drawing

  • Drag a shape from the left (block, IC/MCU, power, connector, sensor, memory, battery, external, decision, board), or double-click the canvas.
  • Hover a block and drag one of its + handles onto another block to connect them; drop on empty space to make a new block already connected.
  • Choose the connection type first: signal, power, ground, digital bus, high-speed/differential, analog, RF or cable. Each has its own colour and line style, and exports include a legend.
  • The panel on the right edits the selected item: name, detail (part number, rating), shape, colour, size, notes; for connections the label (e.g. "I²C 0x48" or "3V3, 500 mA"), type, arrows, route and sides.
  • Select several with Shift-click or by dragging a box, then align or space them evenly. Group frames carry the blocks inside them when moved.
  • Keys: Delete, Ctrl+Z/Y undo/redo, Ctrl+C/V/D, arrow keys to nudge, Alt while dragging turns off grid snapping, Ctrl+S saves.

Unsaved work is kept in your browser, so a closed tab doesn't lose it: you'll be offered to restore it next time.

Versions, review and approval

Save version stores a numbered version (with an optional note). On the diagram's page, Submit for review notifies the project lead. Reviewers comment on the whole diagram or on one block (clicking the comment highlights the block). The lead approves the version or asks for changes. Saving a new version of an approved diagram starts a new draft; the approved version stays on record.

Exports

PDF (A4 or A3, vector, with a title block: product, board, level, version, status, approver, author and date), SVG, PNG and JSON. Every version can be downloaded, and Download everything includes the newest and approved version of every diagram.
