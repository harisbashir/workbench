Purchasing & production

Boards assembled by JLCPCB, Seeed or another assembly house

Most builds are assembled by an assembly house and then finished in house (connectors, through-hole parts, parts the assembler doesn't stock). Workbench splits the work for you:

  1. Mark who fits each part. On the revision's BOM, each line is Assembly house, In house or Do not fit. Through-hole parts are suggested as in house.
  2. Order files. Order files on the build (or the BOM) gives the assembly house's BOM and placement (CPL) files, with in-house and do-not-fit parts left out, plus the Gerbers. Checks warn about parts without an LCSC number or placement.
  3. Plan the build with assembly by an assembly house, then finished in house, and record the order number when you place the order.
  4. Receive the boards. Only the in-house parts are taken from stock — the assembler supplies the rest.
  5. Finish them. The build page becomes a checklist: one step per in-house part, highlighted on the board picture, with +1/+5/All buttons as boards are done. Add your own steps (wash, conformal coat). It works on a phone at the bench. Print traveler gives a paper sheet with a tick box per board.
  6. Test & flash, then record passed/failed boards.

Running a build fully in house

  1. Plan the build. Production → Plan a build. Pick the revision, the number of boards, and the assembly house (or in-house).
  2. Check parts. The build page lists every part needed and what's short.
  3. Order missing parts. Order missing parts creates draft purchase orders for exactly the shortfall, one per supplier. Parts without a supplier are listed so you can set one.
  4. Start the build when the parts are in. This takes the parts out of stock (for example, when you ship a kit to the assembler).
  5. Record results. When boards come back and are tested, enter how many passed and failed. Yield is calculated and posted to the project channel.

Purchase orders

StatusMeansWhat you do next
DraftBeing prepared.Add parts, place the order with the supplier, then Mark as ordered.
OrderedSent to the supplier.When it arrives, enter quantities and Receive into stock.
Partly receivedSome items arrived.Receive the rest when it comes.
ReceivedEverything arrived.Nothing — stock is updated.

Receiving updates each part's unit cost to the price on the order, so BOM costs stay current.

Workbench tracks what was ordered and received; payments and accounting stay in your accounting system.
